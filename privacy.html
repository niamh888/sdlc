Privacy & Data Protection

This page explains what personal information this site handles, where it goes, and what rights you have over it. It is written to be read, not to be skimmed past.

Last updated: 13 September 2026

The short version

  • This site sets no cookies for you as a visitor or learner, uses no analytics, and loads nothing from advertising or tracking companies.
  • What you type into the contact form is used only to reply to you.
  • Contact form messages are handled by Formspree, a service based in the United States.
  • If you create an account, we store your email address, a securely hashed password, and (optionally) your name — used to sign you in and to save your quiz results to your own account.
  • A completed quiz attempt (your name, score and the date) is saved so you can find it again and reprint your certificate — the account, database and backend that store this are all hosted in the EU (Frankfurt, Germany), not transferred elsewhere.
  • A certificate's ID is deliberately enough on its own for anyone to confirm it is genuine, without signing in — see "Accounts, quiz results and certificates" below for what that means in practice.
  • A course review you submit is checked by us before publishing; once approved, your name, star rating and comment are visible to anyone who visits the home page.
  • We never sell or share your information for marketing.

Who is responsible for your information

The data controller — the party legally responsible for the personal information described here — is:

St John Lynch & Co Ltd
Email: niamh@stjohnlynch.com

If you have a question about your information, or you want to exercise any of the rights described further down, that address is the place to write.

The contact form

What we collect

Only what you type in: your name, your email address, optionally your role from the dropdown, and your message. There are no hidden fields collecting anything about you. The form does contain two hidden fields, and it is worth being open about what they are: one sets the subject line of the email we receive, and the other is an empty anti-spam field that automated bots fill in and humans never see.

Why we need it

To read your message and reply to it. Your email address is needed because there is no other way to answer you. Your name is needed to address you properly. That is the entire purpose — your details are not added to a mailing list, and you will not receive anything you did not ask for.

Our legal basis

We rely on legitimate interests under Article 6(1)(f) of the UK and EU GDPR: you have chosen to contact us, and we cannot respond without processing what you sent. If you would rather we did not hold your message at all, say so in your message or write to the address above and we will delete it.

Who else sees it

When you press Send, your message is transmitted to Formspree, a form-handling service, which converts it into an email and delivers it to us. Formspree therefore processes your message on our behalf. Their own privacy policy is published on formspree.io.

This involves a transfer outside the European Economic Area. Formspree is based in the United States, so your name, email address and message are sent to and stored on servers there. We consider this proportionate for a simple contact form, but you are entitled to know it happens before you decide to write to us. If you would prefer your information not to leave the EEA, email us directly at the address above instead of using the form.

Nobody else receives your message. It is not sold, rented, shared for marketing, or used to build a profile of you.

How long we keep it

We delete your message once your query has been dealt with, and in every case no later than 48 hours after we have replied. In practice that means removing both the submission held by Formspree and the email in our inbox. We do not keep contact form messages as a long-term record, and we do not archive them anywhere else.

Information stored in your browser

This site stores small pieces of information on your own device, using a browser feature called local storage. It survives closing the tab and the browser, until you clear it yourself — deliberately, for the progress-tracking items below: they are shared across every tab you have this site open in, which a shorter-lived alternative is not, and losing your progress the moment you closed a tab is not something a course progress tracker should do.

62304_trainingLevel Remembers whether you chose Introductory or Advanced, so you do not have to reselect it on every page

The first five items above do not identify you, stay on your device, and are never transmitted to us or anyone else. The sixth, your sign-in token, is different in exactly the way its description says: it is sent to our backend on every request that needs to know who you are, which is what "being signed in" actually means. All six can be cleared at any time through your browser's "clear browsing data" settings, and the sixth is also removed the moment you log out. No consent banner is required for any of them — the first five exist purely to make the site work as you have already asked it to, and the sixth exists purely to keep you signed in, which is the feature you asked for by logging in, not to track you.

This site sets no cookies for you as a visitor or learner — the table above uses localStorage, a different browser storage mechanism, not cookies (see "Accounts, quiz results and certificates" above for the one cookie this site does set, on an internal admin tool you never encounter).

Accounts, quiz results and certificates

This section used to say the opposite of what follows — quiz results were once kept only in your browser's memory and never sent anywhere. That stopped being true once accounts, saved results and certificate verification were built, and this notice is updated to match, not left describing an earlier version of the site.

What we collect

When you create an account: your email address, a securely hashed password, and optionally your full name. A password hash is a one-way cryptographic transformation — we could not read your actual password even if we wanted to, and it is never logged or stored anywhere in plain text.

Each time you complete a quiz attempt: the name you sat it under, your score, whether you passed, the level (Introductory or Advanced), and the date and time you started and finished it. This is tied to your account.

Why we need it

To let you sign back in, see your own past results on the My Results page, and reprint a certificate at any time without retaking the assessment. A certificate also carries a Certificate ID — see below for what that enables.

Our legal basis

We rely on legitimate interests under Article 6(1)(f) of the UK and EU GDPR: you have chosen to create an account specifically to use this feature, and we cannot provide it — signing you back in, showing you your own results — without keeping this data.

Certificate verification is deliberately public

The Certificate ID printed on every certificate is a long, effectively unguessable code. Anyone who has it — typically because you showed or sent them your certificate — can look up the name, course, score and date it confirms at Verify a Certificate, without needing an account or signing in. This is what lets an employer or auditor confirm a certificate is genuine on their own, which is the whole point of it. It also means that ID is not fully private once it exists: treat it the way you would the certificate itself — something you choose to share, not something we publish or list anywhere ourselves. A certificate for an assessment you did not pass is never available this way; only a genuine pass ever produces a certificate at all.

Course reviews work differently and are covered in their own section below — this verification feature never exposes anything beyond what a certificate itself already shows.

Who processes it, and where

This data is stored in a Postgres database hosted by Neon, and handled by our own backend application (written specifically for this site, not a third-party product) hosted by Render. Both are explicitly configured to run in the EU — Frankfurt, Germany (AWS eu-central-1). Unlike the contact form, no transfer outside the European Economic Area happens for this data — it is both stored and processed inside the EU throughout.

A separate, internal admin tool — used only by us, to check and approve course reviews before they appear publicly (see below) — sets a login cookie of its own on the device used to access it. This is not something you encounter as a visitor or learner; it exists only for our own account, reached at a separate address you are never sent to.

How long we keep it

There is no automatic deletion today — your account, its quiz results, and any reviews you have submitted are kept until you ask us to remove them. See Your rights below for how.

Course reviews

What we collect

The name you give, a star rating from 1 to 5, and the comment you write, submitted from the home page while signed in.

Why we need it, and our legal basis

To show prospective participants real feedback from people who have completed the course. The legal basis is consent under Article 6(1)(a): submitting the review form is a clear, deliberate action, and nothing you write is published without you having taken it.

Moderation, and what becomes public

Every review is checked by us before it can appear — new reviews are never shown automatically. Once approved, your name, star rating and comment are visible to anyone who visits the home page, signed in or not. A review that is rejected, or still awaiting a decision, is never shown to anyone but you.

You can ask for a published review to be removed at any time using the same contact address as every other request on this page — see Your rights below.

Who processes it, and where

The same Render- and Neon-hosted backend described above, in the same EU region — no transfer outside the EEA for reviews either.

Hosting and server logs

The frontend of this site is hosted on GitHub Pages; accounts, quiz results, certificate verification and reviews are handled by our own backend on Render, backed by a database on Neon (both EU-hosted — see above). Like any web server, each of these records technical details of requests, which can include your IP address and which browser you are using. This happens automatically for every website and API you use and is necessary to deliver pages and responses to you. We have no separate access to GitHub's or Render's own infrastructure logs beyond what each provider publishes under its own privacy statement, and do not use them to look anyone up.

What this site does not do

  • No cookies for you as a visitor or learner (the one exception — our own internal admin login — is covered above, and is not something you ever encounter)
  • No analytics or visitor statistics — we genuinely do not know how many people use this site, only how many accounts and quiz attempts exist
  • No advertising, tracking pixels, or third-party scripts of any kind
  • No external fonts or content delivery networks — every visual asset (HTML, CSS, JavaScript, images) comes from this site; the only other systems it talks to are its own backend (Render and Neon, both EU-hosted) and, for the contact form only, Formspree
  • No selling, renting, or sharing of personal information for marketing — a course review is the one thing you provide that is published, and only once you have submitted it and we have approved it, exactly as described above
  • No automated decision-making or profiling

Your rights

Under data protection law you have the right to:

  • Access — ask for a copy of the personal information we hold about you
  • Rectification — have inaccurate information corrected
  • Erasure — ask us to delete your information
  • Restriction — ask us to limit how we use it
  • Object — object to our processing where we rely on legitimate interests
  • Portability — receive your information in a transferable format

To exercise any of these, email niamh@stjohnlynch.com. We will respond within one month. Depending on what you have used the site for, we may hold a contact form message, an account (email, name, password hash), your quiz results, or a course review you have submitted — say which applies, or ask for all of it, and we will act on whichever is actually there. There is no self-service "delete my account" button on the site today; a deletion request is handled by us by hand on receiving your email.

If you are unhappy with how we have handled your information, you can complain to the Irish supervisory authority, the Data Protection Commission (dataprotection.ie).

Where this site's content comes from

Accuracy matters more here than on most training sites, because someone may carry a conclusion from this course into a real regulatory submission. So it is worth being explicit about what came from where.

  • The course content is written by St John Lynch & Co Ltd, based on a licensed copy of IEC 62304:2006+AMD1:2015. The text of the standard is not reproduced here — requirements are paraphrased, and clause numbers are cited so you can look them up in your own copy.
  • The safety class mapping (which requirements apply to Class A, B or C) is taken from Table A.1 as amended and cross-checked line by line against the [Class …] tags in the normative text. The mapping carries the date it was last checked, and records the single entry that rests on inference rather than an explicit tag, so you can see exactly how solid each value is.
  • The Edition 2 status information comes from the publicly available IEC TC 62 work programme and TC 62 committee drafts. It is shown with the date it was last reviewed, because a standard under development moves and an undated claim about it quietly becomes wrong.

This site is not affiliated with, authorised by, or endorsed by the IEC. "IEC" and the standard designations referred to here belong to the International Electrotechnical Commission. Where this site and the standard disagree, the standard governs — check your own copy, and check the IEC website for the current development status before relying on anything said here about Edition 2.

One point that is a data protection matter: your browser never contacts the IEC, or any other third party, when you use this site. Everything the page loads comes from this site. Where information about the standard's development status is drawn from IEC, that happens away from your visit and is published here as an ordinary file, so no request of yours and no IP address of yours reaches IEC.
